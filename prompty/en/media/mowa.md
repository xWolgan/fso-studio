# Speech

## Medium: speech (voice and text)

The prompt has two parts: a **voice description** and the **text to be spoken**.

### Voice description — matrix

1. **Recording situation** — where, through what, in what convention (e.g. "an interview for a 1990s radio documentary, recorded in the yard in front of the house"). It sets the acoustics and the manner of speaking. ← Voice and word, World and interiors, Time and rhythm.
2. **Who** — gender, age, origin, accent (or its absence), timbre, "instrument". ← Characters and people.
3. **Texture of the voice** — huskiness, a tremor on drawn-out vowels, breath — concretely.
4. **Character** — in one sentence.
5. **Relation to what is being said** — prepared or off the cuff, who they are speaking to, what state they are in.
6. **Manner of speaking** — pace, pauses, stumbles, fillers, sudden stresses, breathing, throat-clearing.

### Text to be spoken

- The exact wording. Numbers, dates and abbreviations — spelled out in words.
- **Punctuation steers prosody**: comma — short pause; ellipsis — suspension; dash — a break-off; full stop — a fall.
- The poem: verbatim, unless the participant wants otherwise.

### Translation rules

- **A series of voices**: shared sentences (recording situation, description of unprepared speech) you repeat verbatim; you vary only the traits of the person.
- Description length: about 1000 characters worked well (Channelers) — check the limit and format in the model card.
- **Voice clone**: the prompt is text + a sample; a description of traits works differently or not at all — see the card.
- Describe with concrete traits, not evaluations ("a low, husky voice, wrecked by cigarettes", not "an interesting voice").
